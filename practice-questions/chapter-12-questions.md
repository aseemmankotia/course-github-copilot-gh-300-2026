## Chapter 12: GH-300 Full Practice Exam Simulation and Time-Management Strategy — Practice Questions

**Q1.** You are 40 questions into the 65-question GH-300 exam with 45 minutes remaining on the clock. You are unsure about the current question. What is the BEST action?

A) Select your best guess, flag the question for review, and move on immediately
B) Spend up to 10 minutes working through the question until you are certain of the answer
C) Skip the question entirely and leave it blank so you can return later
D) End the exam early and reschedule for a day when you feel more prepared

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: A**

Selecting a best guess guarantees a chance at credit if you run out of time to return, while flagging preserves the option to revisit if time allows. This protects your pace of roughly 1.5 minutes per question.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Spending 10 minutes on one question blows your pacing budget by 6-7x and risks not reaching later questions
- Leaving it blank guarantees zero credit if you never make it back, unlike flagging with a guess already locked in
- Ending the exam forfeits the attempt and any partial score progress; this is not a pacing decision

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q2.** The GH-300 exam allots 100 minutes for 65 questions. Approximately how much time should you budget per question to finish with buffer time for a final review pass?

A) About 1.2 to 1.5 minutes per question
B) About 3 minutes per question
C) About 2.5 minutes per question
D) About 45 seconds per question

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: A**

100 minutes / 65 questions is roughly 1.54 minutes per question at the raw average; pacing at 1.2-1.5 minutes on most questions leaves a buffer of several minutes for flagged-question review at the end.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- 3 minutes per question would consume the full 100 minutes by question 33, leaving no time for the rest
- 2.5 minutes per question also overruns the exam before reaching the final third of questions
- 45 seconds per question is unrealistically fast and leaves an excessive, unusable time surplus that encourages rushing and careless errors

</details>

**Q3.** You finish the GH-300 exam and receive a scaled score of 720. What does this score mean?

A) You answered 72% of raw questions correctly
B) You failed, since most GitHub exams require a scaled score of 750 or higher
C) The score is provisional and will be recalculated after manual review
D) You scored above the passing threshold of 700 on a 300-1000 scaled range

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: D**

GH-300 uses a scaled score between 300 and 1000, with 700 as the passing cutoff. A 720 is a pass, and the number is not a direct percentage of raw questions answered correctly.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- The scaled score is not a raw percentage — different questions can carry different weight in the scaling algorithm
- 700, not 750, is the passing threshold for GH-300 style scaled scoring
- Scaled scores for multiple-choice/multi-select formatted exams are typically final at test completion, not provisional pending manual review

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q4.** During your final review week before sitting the GH-300 exam, you have limited study hours left. Which domain review order best reflects domain-weighted priority?

A) Review every domain for an equal number of hours regardless of weight
B) Review the lightest-weighted domains first since they are quicker to finish
C) Review the heaviest-weighted domain (Plans and Features) first, then work down by weight
D) Review domains in the order they appear in the course outline, ignoring weight

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: C**

With limited time, prioritizing the heaviest-weighted domain first maximizes expected score improvement per hour studied, since it represents the largest share of scored questions.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Equal time allocation wastes hours on light domains at the expense of the domain most likely to appear on the exam
- Starting with lightest domains delays coverage of the highest-impact material and risks running out of time before reaching it
- Course outline order is a logical teaching sequence, not an exam-weight-optimized review sequence

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q5.** What is the correct purpose of the 'flag for review' feature available during the GH-300 exam?

A) It marks a question so you can quickly return to it during a second pass without hunting for it manually
B) It automatically skips the question and removes it from your score
C) It sends the question to GitHub support for a content accuracy dispute
D) It pauses the exam timer until you unflag the question

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: A**

Flagging bookmarks a question in the navigation panel so you can jump directly back to it later, supporting a first-pass/second-pass pacing strategy.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Flagging does not remove the question from scoring; an answer you selected before flagging still counts if you never return
- Flagging is a personal navigation aid, not a mechanism for disputing question content
- The exam timer runs continuously regardless of flagged questions

</details>

**Q6.** With 5 minutes left on the clock, you still have 8 unanswered questions. What is the BEST use of remaining time?

A) Leave the remaining questions blank to avoid a penalty for wrong guesses
B) Spend all 5 minutes carefully solving just 2 of the 8 questions in depth
C) Restart the exam section to get a fresh time allocation
D) Quickly select an answer for every remaining question, even if it is only a guess

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: D**

There is no penalty for an incorrect answer on GH-300, so submitting a guess for every remaining question maximizes expected score versus leaving items blank.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Leaving answers blank guarantees zero credit on those items when a guess has a nonzero chance of being correct
- Deeply solving only 2 of 8 questions abandons the other 6 to guaranteed zero credit when quick guesses could have scored on any of them
- There is no mechanism to restart a section and reset the timer during a live certification attempt

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q7.** A common last-minute mistake reported by GH-300 test-takers involves the final 10-15 questions of the exam. What is this trap?

A) Test-takers spend too much time re-reading the exam instructions again near the end
B) Test-takers are required to retake the Responsible AI domain questions a second time
C) Test-takers rush through final questions without reading scenario details carefully because they are anxious about the clock
D) The exam interface automatically shortens the remaining questions to multiple-choice-only format

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: C**

Clock anxiety near the end commonly causes skimming of scenario-based question stems, leading to avoidable errors on questions the candidate could otherwise answer correctly.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Re-reading instructions is not a documented common trap and is not a normal exam behavior at that stage
- There is no mechanic where a domain's questions repeat within the same exam attempt
- The exam does not dynamically change question formats based on time remaining

</details>

**Q8.** Before scheduling your GH-300 exam attempt, what should you do regarding the exam structure described in your study materials?

A) Trust that question count, timing, and passing score never change once published
B) Verify current question count, time limit, and passing score on the official GitHub certifications page, since exam specifics can be updated over time
C) Assume the practice exam simulation exactly matches the live exam question-for-question
D) Skip verification since GitHub does not publish exam structure details publicly

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

Certification exam structures (question counts, durations, and passing scores) can be revised by the certifying body, so candidates should confirm current details on the official page shortly before scheduling.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Exam structures are not permanently fixed; providers do update format details across exam versions
- Practice simulations approximate the live exam's style and difficulty but never reuse the actual question bank verbatim
- GitHub does publish exam structure details (domains, question count, duration) on its official certifications page

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>
